L809@P7: 5.1 Who is Using Our Code?
L810@P7: In a company that produces both applications and frameworks, a
L811@P7: framework team may not be aware of every other individual or team
L812@P7: who is using their framework. This makes it difficult to notify dependents when breaking changes must be made (information needs
L813@P7: #6 and #10 on our inter-team coordination survey). Codebook can
L814@P7: be used to mitigate this issue by discovering everyone who may
L815@P7: be affected by breaking changes, e.g., by discovering when a person (in the team) edited some source code which is called by code
L816@P7: edited by another person (outside the team):
L817@P7: ∙ Person Committed ChangeSet Modifies FileRevision
L818@P7: Modifies SourceCode CalledBy SourceCode NamedBy
L819@P7: SourceCode ModifiedBy FileRevision ModifiedBy
L820@P7: ChangeSet CommittedBy Person
L821@P7: Once these paths are computed, Codebook can easily filter the
L822@P7: results of this regular expression to restrict the person at the beginning of the path to be people inside a team, and the person at the
L823@P7-8: end at the path to be people outside that team. The user interfacewould also provide action items, such as a link for contacting the
L824@P8: owners of all calling methods in order to inform them of breaking
L825@P8: changes.
L826@P8: 5.2 Anxious for Awareness
L827@P8: When teams collaborate as part of a large project, a member of
L828@P8: one team will often assign a work item to a member of another
L829@P8: team. Tracking the status of work items assigned across teams is
L830@P8: frustrating because the teams’ independent work is not transparent
L831@P8: to each other (information need #5 from our survey). The work
L832@P8: item can be delayed due to poor communication, differing priorities, or forgotten altogether because no one advocates for it [7].
