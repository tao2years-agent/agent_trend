Frequently asked questions

How does seat-based billing work?
You only pay for active contributors. A contributor is active if they open a pull request or push commits to one in the last 30 days. Inactive teammates, bots, and one-off contributors don't count, and you can see exactly who counts in your dashboard.
What's the difference between monthly and yearly billing?
Yearly billing on the Max plan saves 15% compared to paying monthly. You can switch between monthly and yearly at any time.
Is there really a free plan?
Yes. Mergify is free for open source projects and for private teams of up to 5 active contributors. You get all three products: Merge Queue, CI Insights, and Test Engine. The Open Source and Free plans don't cover the same features, so check the table above for what each one includes.
What do I get on every plan?
Every plan includes all three products: CI Insights, Test Engine, and Merge Queue. We don't gate whole products behind higher tiers. What changes between plans is seats, data retention, support, enterprise controls, and some advanced features inside each product.
Can a large team evaluate Mergify without talking to sales?
Yes. Start a 14-day trial of Max from your dashboard: one click, no credit card, and the full product running on your real repositories. You only need to talk to us if you want more than 100 seats, on-premise deployment, a signed MSA, or a company-wide agreement instead of per-seat billing, and none of that has to happen before you try it.
When should I talk to sales about Enterprise?
Enterprise fits when you need more than 100 seats, on-premise deployment, custom data retention, a signed MSA, 24/7 premium support, or quarterly roadmap reviews. On-premise means Mergify Enterprise Server: the full product running inside your own network, connected to GitHub Enterprise Server or GitHub.com. Pricing is custom and volume-based, so book a call and we'll size it with you.
Do you offer discounts for startups?
Yes. Our Startup Program gives eligible startups up to $12,000 in credit so you can skip the CI chaos while you're still finding product-market fit.
